- How the app looks, and the rules that keep it looking the same. The UI is [[The UI]]; the pieces are built with [[Tailwind CSS]].
- Colours have names for what they are for, not what they are
	- `apps/ui/app/globals.css` declares each as a CSS variable with a light and a dark value, and exposes it to Tailwind: `bg-surface`, `text-ink-muted`, `border-border`. A component never writes a palette colour (`slate-500`) or a `dark:` pair, so changing a theme is changing one value.
	- Light is the default and dark follows the system's setting. There is no switch.
	- The values are Tailwind's slate, with sky for links, red for errors and amber for flags.
- Grounds
	- `surface` is the page, the main pane and cards. `surface-pane` is the sidebars: a step darker in light and lighter in dark. `surface-hover` is hover, the current item, chips and code.
- Three levels of text
	- `ink` for content, `ink-secondary` for secondary text and icons on any ground, `ink-muted` for hints, and only on `surface`: on the other grounds it is under 4.5:1 for contrast.
- Colour is rare
	- `link`, `danger`, `flag-text` and `flag-ring`, and the five `sync-*` dots, which are always paired with a label so that colour is never the only signal. [[Sync status]]
- Borders, not shadows
	- `border` is the hairline between panes, cards and rows. `border-control` is the edge of a control that needs one.
- The shared pieces are in `apps/ui/components/ui.tsx`
	- `Button` is outlined and never filled; `danger` is for what is asked about first, and `overlay` floats over the graph. `Input` is the one text field, `ErrorLine` the one way to say what went wrong, `Kbd` a key hint, and `iconButtonClass` the small button in a card's header or a toolbar.
	- `sectionHeading` is the small-capitals caption, and `paneHeading` the same on a sidebar's ground, where the muted colour is too faint. `linkClass` is a link, and `unresolvedLinkClass` one to somewhere that doesn't exist yet.
	- A screen uses these rather than typing the classes again, so a change to how a button looks is made once.
- Two kinds of hover
	- A control (a button, a row, a table row) is small and sought out, so hovering it fills it. The "+ Add a block" row is wide and usually invisible, so the pointer crosses it all the time without meaning to use it; it only gets an outline, which says it is there without a heavy block flashing across the page.
- Focus, touch and motion
	- Every control shows where the keyboard is with one 2px ring in the link colour (`focus-ring`), drawn by a base rule in `globals.css`. A field that draws its own focus, such as the search box and the editor, says so with its own outline.
	- On a touch screen (`coarse`, a Tailwind variant for `pointer: coarse`) buttons, fields, sidebar rows and search hits are at least 44px tall; with a mouse, controls are at least 32px.
	- Nothing is only there on hover. `no-hover` is the variant for a device that can't hover, and it is why the "+ Add a block" row is simply shown on a touch screen. [[Editing]]
	- A person who has asked for less motion gets none: the drawer doesn't slide, the placeholders don't pulse and the graph doesn't glide to its frame.
- Type and widths
	- Text is [[IBM Plex Sans]] and code is [[JetBrains Mono]] in its no-ligatures build.
	- The entity page reads at a fluid size, at least 20px, and sizes what is inside it (its title at 1.5 times, chips smaller) in `em`. Every other page reads at 16px and chrome at 14px. A page's title is 24px with one space under it, a section within a page 18px, and a heading inside a section 16px; all are semibold.
	- A page's text is one of three widths: reading (50rem), a form (56rem) or a table (72rem), named `max-w-measure-text`, `-form` and `-table`.
- Layers that float
	- The search panel, the link list in the editor and the right pane when it overlays all look the same: the page's ground, a hairline border, a rounded corner and one shadow (`shadow-overlay`). Where something blocks the page there is a dim layer under it. What covers what is four named levels: the overlaid pane, the dim layer, the drawer and whatever floats over everything (`--z-pane`, `--z-scrim`, `--z-drawer`, `--z-overlay`).
- Colours that Tailwind can't reach follow the theme too
	- The graph's canvas reads its colours from the stylesheet (`--graph-link`, `--graph-node` and so on) and asks again when the scheme changes, and the editor's selection is `--selection`. Nothing outside `globals.css` writes a colour.
